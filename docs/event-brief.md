# Event brief

## What Kelingkong is

An orientation event for Indonesian university students in Hong Kong. Students are
introduced to Hong Kong by exploring places all across the city. Because the intake is
large — around 200–250 students — participants are split into groups, which also means
freshmen get to know each other naturally rather than through forced icebreakers.

## Who it serves

**Participants:** incoming Indonesian university students in Hong Kong, most of them new
to the city and to each other. Around 250 expected in 2026.

**Group leaders:** helpers drawn from ranting and PPI HK, two per team. They travel with
their team all day.

**Game post staff:** a separate pool of 9, stationed at three fixed posts.

## What success looks like

Three goals, in the order they should win when they conflict:

1. **Freshmen leave knowing people.** The grouping is the mechanism; anything that keeps
   teams isolated all day is working against the point of the event.
2. **Students see parts of Hong Kong they wouldn't have found alone.** Not a checklist of
   landmarks — enough time at a place to actually be there.
3. **The day is enjoyable.** In 2025 this is where it slipped: the schedule was tight
   enough that exploring became a race.

Goal 3 is why the 2026 format changed. See `2025/retrospective.md` for what went wrong
and `2026/concept.md` for what replaced it.

## Shape of the day

Roughly 10:00 to 16:30. Teams assemble, disperse across the city to explore in their
groups, break for lunch wherever they happen to be, and regroup at the end for closing
and results. Around 4.5 hours of that is live exploration once assembly, lunch and
closing are subtracted — the number every pacing decision is derived from.

Base and regroup point in 2025 was BNI Admiralty; travel times in `data/landmarks.csv`
are measured from there.

## Constraints that shape everything

- **Volunteers, not participants, are the limit.** Team count follows how many group
  leaders can be recruited. This was 2025's finding and it still holds.
- **Hong Kong in September is typhoon and rainstorm season.** A wet-weather plan is not
  optional; 2025 had none.
- **Everything happens on public transport, spread across the city.** Teams are
  unsupervised beyond their two leaders, so mechanics have to work without a referee
  present.
