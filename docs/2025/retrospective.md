# Kelingkong 2025 — retrospective

## What the committee wrote down

Rows 54–59 of the *Rules & Point System* tab, verbatim and translated:

| Original | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `Group terlalu rame (di dalem grup dipecah jadi 2)` | Groups too crowded — splitting each group into two sub-teams was floated |
| `Terlalu kompetetif (gabisa di enjoy) mau dibikin santai` | Too competitive to actually enjoy; wanted it more relaxed |
| `Volunteer (kalo lebih banyak pos)` | More volunteers needed if there are more posts |
| `group lebih banyak kalau volunteer banyak` | More groups possible only if there are more volunteers |
| `Plan B ujan` | Need a rain plan B |

## Reading the four complaints

### 1. Pace — the dominant one

"Too competitive to enjoy" is really a pacing complaint. 2025 scored by *visiting*, and
every landmark visited added points with no diminishing return, so the optimal play was
to cover as much ground as possible in the time available. The format rewarded hurrying,
and hurrying is what people remembered.

Nothing in the rules capped how much a group should attempt. There was no point at which
a group could reasonably say "we have enough, let's sit down."

**This is what the 2026 redesign targets.** See `../2026/concept.md`.

### 2. Group size

10 per group. Large enough that the group photo requirement — *every* member in frame —
became an act of logistics, and large enough that quieter members could disappear into
the crowd rather than meet anyone.

Not fixed by any 2026 mechanic. Team size is a function of how many leaders get
recruited, so it is a recruitment decision, not a design one. 2026 holds at 10 per team;
getting to 8–9 would need 28+ teams and ~56 leaders.

### 3. Volunteers as the binding constraint

Written down twice, in both directions: more posts need more volunteers, and more groups
are only possible with more volunteers. The committee already understood that volunteer
recruitment gates everything else.

2026 respects this by holding post staff at 9 and designing the post format to fit that
number rather than assuming growth.

### 4. No rain plan

Kelingkong runs in September, which is typhoon and rainstorm season, and the entire event
is outdoors and dispersed across the city. 2025 had no documented contingency.

Tracked as an open item in `../2026/operations.md`.

## What the data reveals that the notes don't

**Zone totals were badly unbalanced.** Available points per region: HK Island 33, TST 44,
Kai Tak 70. Kai Tak was worth more than double Hong Kong Island.

This was probably deliberate — Kai Tak landmarks sat 33–45 minutes from base against 5–18
for Hong Kong Island, so the extra points compensated for travel time. But combined with
the +30 "visit every region" bonus, it created strong pressure to make the long trip to
Kai Tak, which fed the sense of a race against the clock.

**Manual scoring never scaled.** The *Score Sheet* tab was built out for Groups 1, 2 and
3 — of 21. Whatever happened for the remaining 18 groups is not recorded. Tallying 21
groups by hand across landmarks, posts, bonuses and surprise missions was never a solved
problem, and it is the single operational failure most likely to repeat.

**The finer district structure already existed and went unused.** The *Landmarks* tab
groups landmarks by MTR district — a much better structure than the three coarse regions
actually used for scoring. 2026 builds on it directly.
